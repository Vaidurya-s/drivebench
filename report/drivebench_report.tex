\begin{finding}[title=Summary]
Four controllers regulate the shaft speed of one simulated motor through one shared voltage drive.
They see identical references, load torques, bus levels and encoder noise in eleven runs.
MPC has the lowest speed-error integral (IAE) in \Lw{winsMPC} of the 11 runs, LQR in \Lw{winsLQR}, and \Lw{winsTie} is a tie (within 0.5\,\%);
LQR is the fastest to settle at startup (\M{motor_startup}{lqr}{settling_time_s}\,s) and the best at
load disturbances (IAE \M{load_step}{lqr}{iae} against \M{load_step}{mpc}{iae} for MPC);
MPC keeps the drive out of its current limit for less than half the time of the others under
overload (\M{current_protection}{mpc}{current_limit_s}\,s against \M{current_protection}{lqr}{current_limit_s}\,s for LQR) but
costs about \M{motor_startup}{mpc}{exec_ms_mean}\,ms of computation per 10\,ms sample instead of
\M{motor_startup}{lqr}{exec_ms_mean}\,ms.
The LQR and MPC settings were chosen by hand while looking at these runs, so this is a
demonstration of mechanisms, not a neutral tuning contest.
\end{finding}

\section{The drive and what limits it}
A speed drive of this kind turns conveyors, pumps and machine axes. The controller does not touch the
motor directly: it requests an armature voltage, and a drive layer decides what the motor actually
receives. Figure~\ref{fig:loop} shows the signal path used for every controller in this report.

The drive applies, in this order: an enable/e-stop gate, a slew limit of \SI{1200}{\volt\per\second}, saturation at $\pm V_{\max}$ times the
DC-bus level (24\,V nominal), and a current window. The window is not a clip of the simulated current. It restricts the
voltage so that the exact one-sample electrical response stays inside $\pm I_{\max}$ (8\,A), using the drive's nominal $R_a$ and $K_e$ and
the measured current and speed. Under $\pm20\,\%$ winding-resistance error it can be exceeded briefly (up to \M{model_mismatch_low}{lqr}{max_current_a}\,A in the
low-resistance corner) before the feedback term pulls the voltage down.

\section{The motor model}
The plant is the coupled electrical and mechanical system
\begin{equation}
L_a\frac{di_a}{dt}=V_a-R_a i_a-K_e\omega,\qquad J\frac{d\omega}{dt}=K_t i_a-B\omega-T_L,\qquad \frac{d\theta}{dt}=\omega.
\end{equation}
With the baseline parameters in Table~\ref{tab:params} the state matrices for $x=[i_a,\omega,\theta]^\top$ are
\begin{equation}
A=\begin{bmatrix}-100&-5&0\\25&-0.5&0\\0&1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad
B=\begin{bmatrix}100\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad
E=\begin{bmatrix}0\\-500\\0\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
and the transfer function $\Omega/V_a=K_t/(L_aJs^2+(L_aB+R_aJ)s+R_aB+K_eK_t)$ has poles at $-98.7$ and $-1.75\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$:
an electrical time constant of 10\,ms and a mechanical one of about 0.57\,s. The plant is integrated with RK4 at 1\,ms while the
controllers update every 10\,ms and hold their output between updates.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\caption{Baseline motor and drive parameters (illustrative, not a commercial motor).}\label{tab:params}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{\hspace{1.4cm}}lr@{}}
\toprule
$R_a$ & 1.0\,$\Omega$ & $J$ & 0.002\,kg\,m$^2$ \\
$L_a$ & 10\,mH & $B$ & 0.001\,N\,m\,s/rad \\
$K_t=K_e$ & 0.05 & nominal $T_L$ & 0.05\,N\,m \\

\textbf{Four models, kept apart.} The \emph{actual plant} may use mismatched $R_a,L_a,J,B$. The controllers' \emph{prediction models} are always the
nominal motor. The \emph{drive} is a separate layer with its own limits. The \emph{measurement model} adds seeded Gaussian noise to the encoder and
measures current ideally.

\subsection{PID and optimised PID}
The positional PID takes RPM and returns volts. Its derivative acts on the (low-pass filtered) measurement so reference steps cause no kick~\cite{ah2006}, and the
integrator is unwound by back-calculation against the voltage the drive \emph{applied}, so slew, saturation and current limiting all stop windup.
The hand-tuned gains are $K_p{=}0.01$\,V/RPM, $K_i{=}0.03$, $K_d{=}0$. The optimised PID uses the same law with gains minimising a weighted sum of IAE,
overshoot, settling time, voltage effort and time in voltage saturation and current limit (L-BFGS-B). They were found once on the
speed-tracking scenario ($K_p{=}\Gp{kp}$, $K_i{=}\Gp{ki}$, $K_d{=}\Gp{kd}$) and are frozen for every comparison.

\subsection{LQR with integral action}
Augmenting the state with $\xi=\int(\omega-r)\,dt$ gives a three-state design model. The plant part is discretised exactly (zero-order hold) at
$T_s=10$\,ms, and the integrator uses the same forward-Euler update the controller executes, so the Riccati design certifies the loop that actually runs.
The cost is $\sum x_{aug}^\top Qx_{aug}+u^\top Ru$ on states scaled by $I_{\max}$, $\omega_{\max}$, $0.5\,\mathrm{s}\cdot\omega_{\max}$ and voltage scaled by $V_{\max}$,
with dimensionless weights $q_i=\Lw{q_current}$, $q_\omega=\Lw{q_speed}$, $q_\xi=\Lw{q_integral}$, $r_V=\Lw{r_voltage}$.
Solving the discrete algebraic Riccati equation~\cite{am1990} gives the gain
\begin{equation}
K=\bigl[K_i,K_\omega,K_\xi\bigr]=\bigl[\Lw{KI},\ \Lw{KW},\ \Lw{KX}\bigr]\ \text{(V/A, V/(rad/s), V/rad)},
\end{equation}
and the law is $u=u_{ff}(r)-K_x(x-x_{eq}(r))-K_\xi\xi$ with the zero-load equilibrium as feedforward. The design is rejected, and no gain used, when the augmented
pair is uncontrollable, the Riccati equation fails or any closed-loop pole leaves the unit circle. Figure~\ref{fig:poles} shows the result: the electrical
pole moves in to about $0.27$ and all closed-loop poles lie within $|z|\le\Lw{maxpole}$.

\subsection{Offset-free MPC}
MPC predicts with the two-state model $x_{k+1}=A_dx_k+B_d(V_k+\hat d_V)+E_d\hat d_T$, $x=[i_a,\omega]^\top$. Each sample the innovations of the
measured current and speed are mapped to a load-torque estimate $\hat d_T$ and an armature-voltage estimate $\hat d_V$ (observer gain 0.1).
This follows the offset-free disturbance-model idea of~\cite{pr2003} within the framework of~\cite{rmd2017}. Estimating both matters: with a torque-only estimate the speed kept a $44$\,RPM offset under $+20\,\%$ resistance in development.
Over a horizon of $N=60$ samples (0.6\,s) it chooses six blocked voltage moves minimising weighted speed error, a terminal speed error and voltage change,
subject to the voltage box, the voltage change between moves, and the \emph{predicted} armature current $|i_{a,k}|\le I_{\max}$ over the whole horizon
(Figure~\ref{fig:horizon}). SciPy SLSQP solves it with analytic gradients because all constraints are linear in the moves.

\subsection{Current protection}
When the load needs more than 8\,A, nothing can hold 2500\,RPM, and the question becomes how the drive spends its time at the limit.
Figure~\ref{fig:limit} shows the intervals during which the drive's current window was active.
MPC leaves the limit for most of the second half (\M{current_protection}{mpc}{current_limit_s}\,s in total), while the other three stay in it
(\M{current_protection}{pid}{current_limit_s}, \M{current_protection}{optimized_pid}{current_limit_s} and \M{current_protection}{lqr}{current_limit_s}\,s), because only MPC sees the limit in its prediction.
Their error integrals are nonetheless within 2\,\% of each other (\M{current_protection}{pid}{iae}, \M{current_protection}{optimized_pid}{iae}, \M{current_protection}{lqr}{iae}, \M{current_protection}{mpc}{iae}), since the actuator, not the algorithm, sets the best achievable speed.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item The LQR weights (chosen from a short sweep on startup) and the MPC horizon, weights and observer gain were set by hand while looking at these runs.
\item LQR and MPC use measured current and a nominal model that matches the plant when no mismatch is applied; the PID loops are single speed loops.
\item The motor is linear with constant parameters: no brush drop, saturation, temperature drift, Coulomb friction or cogging. The current sensor is ideal and the load keeps its sign at standstill.
\item Efficiency figures use an illustrative 0.95 power-stage efficiency and are not data.
\item No stability proof is claimed for any controller under saturation, and the MPC solve time is not a real-time guarantee.
\item The parameters are illustrative; none of the numbers describe a commercial motor.
\end{itemize}
